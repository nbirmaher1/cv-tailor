# CV industry standards (2026)

What actually gets a CV parsed correctly by an ATS, read past a recruiter's 6-8 second scan, and
picked over other qualified candidates for *this* posting. Read this before tailoring content
(step 4) — the review subagent in step 5 checks the draft against every rule here, and step 8
re-checks length against the rendered output. This file is the single canonical source for every
content rule — SKILL.md's steps apply and check these rules but don't restate them, so if a rule
changes, this is the only place it needs to change.

## Structure & length
- Standard section headings only: "Summary", "Experience", "Education", "Skills" — never creative
  alternatives.
- Reverse-chronological order, most recent first.
- **Length is driven by years of relevant experience, not preference: under 10 years → strictly 1
  page. 10+ years → exactly 2 pages.** A ~1.5-page result is never acceptable — cut to 1 page, or
  the experience genuinely earns a full 2.
- **Years of relevant experience**: total professional years in roles substantively related to the
  target role's field or function, per this job posting — not raw career length. Exclude clearly
  unrelated early-career years from the count; keep adjacent/transferable roles in it. If genuinely
  ambiguous, default to the candidate's total professional years rather than guessing low — a
  2-page result for a borderline case reads better than an artificially thin 1-page one.
- **Thin-experience exception**: if the candidate's genuinely relevant material — even uncut —
  doesn't fill a full page (new graduate, single short role, career switcher), landing under a page
  is correct. Never pad with filler bullets, invented detail, or stretched whitespace to force a
  full page.
- Always a text-based document, never an image-based PDF or other non-parseable format.

## Relevance, prioritization, and duplication (the core judgment call)
Every decision below is measured against the same yardstick: the 8-10 most critical
skills/tools/responsibilities extracted from the job posting (SKILL.md step 4 builds this list
first). Everything here depends on it.

- **Role triage**: classify every role core / tangential / irrelevant against the target role. Cut
  irrelevant roles entirely, no matter how impressive, if they don't serve this application.
- **Bullet relevance test**: keep a bullet only if it passes one of two tests — (a) it visibly
  supports one of the extracted requirements, or (b) it's an unambiguous scope/seniority/impact
  signal (team size, revenue, headcount, scale) even without exact keyword overlap. A well-written
  bullet that fails both tests still gets cut.
- **Bullet caps: 3-5 for core roles, 1-2 for tangential/older roles.**
- **Ranking when bullets compete for a capped slot**, in this order: (1) direct requirement match,
  (2) strongest quantifiable outcome, (3) most recent/senior scope, (4) not already covered by
  another kept bullet. Cut from the bottom of this ranking — never by instinct or how well a
  bullet happens to read.
- **Order surviving bullets strongest-and-most-relevant first** within each role. Recruiters and
  ATS reviewers skim top-down; don't default to the source CV's original bullet order.
- **Zero tolerance for duplicate bullets.** For every role, compare each bullet against every other
  bullet in that same role: do they reference the same underlying deliverable, project, or system —
  even from a different angle (e.g. one about *building/creating* it, another about
  *owning/driving/scaling* it)? That's one achievement, not two. Merge into a single bullet using
  the strongest verb and metric from either. A duplicate isn't a harmless repeat — it silently
  costs the role a slot that should hold a genuinely different achievement.
- **Near-duplicates across different roles** aren't automatically a violation — showing the same
  kind of impact at two jobs can demonstrate a real track record — but never restate the identical
  achievement twice. If two roles cover the same project, keep it where it's strongest and either
  drop it from the other role or make the two bullets clearly distinct (different facet of the
  work, different outcome).
- Cut whole irrelevant roles, or trim tangential ones further, when stronger material already fills
  the page — a shorter, focused CV beats a complete but unfocused one.
- **If still over the page limit after selection**, cut in this order: Skills to its cap →
  tangential/older bullets to their cap → core-role bullets, dropping from the bottom of the
  ranking above.

## Content quality (bullet-level writing)
- **Skills-based, not credential-based**: don't just list a tool in Skills — prove it inside an
  achievement bullet wherever the CV supports it. Skills is a supplement, not the primary evidence.
- **Every bullet: strong action verb + what you did + measurable outcome** (%, $, time, volume,
  headcount). "Responsible for managing client accounts" is a duty; "Increased retention 22%
  through data-driven account management" is an achievement. Cut bullets that can't be rewritten
  this way and don't serve the job posting.
- **Tense**: past roles in past tense throughout; the current role in present tense throughout.
  Never mix tenses within a single role's bullets.
- **Plain, accurate verbs — never grandiose ones.** Avoid inflated corporate-buzzword verbs a
  person wouldn't actually say about their own work out loud ("spearheaded," "orchestrated,"
  "championed," "revolutionized," "pioneered," "masterminded"). Use direct, concrete verbs that
  state what was actually done ("built," "led," "reduced," "managed," "analyzed," "launched,"
  "negotiated"). The bar is a bullet a human reads once and immediately understands — not one that
  sounds impressive but is actually vague. Clarity and readability beat sounding amazing.
- **Exact metrics only** — carry over the number from the source verbatim, never invent or round
  up. Translate technical detail into business impact only where the source itself states that
  outcome — don't infer a business result the CV didn't claim.
- **Hard cap: ~2 rendered lines per bullet (~180-200 characters / 28-32 words).** Condense long
  source bullets or narrative paragraphs to fit rather than carrying them over verbatim.
- **The professional summary must be re-tailored every time**: 3-4 tight sentences, mirror the
  target title, name genuinely-supported competencies from the extracted requirements list, include
  one concrete proof point (metric/scale/outcome) from the CV — never reuse generic filler.
- Weave the job posting's real terminology into bullets themselves, not just a keyword dump in
  Skills — only where the CV genuinely backs it up. Adopting JD terminology the CV doesn't support
  is keyword-stuffing, not tailoring.

## Formatting (already enforced by the templates — don't fight them)
- Single-column layout only. Multi-column layouts cause ATS parsers to scramble text order.
- No tables, text boxes, icons, or graphics. The one narrow exception is an optional headshot
  photo — candidate's choice (some regions expect it); it can introduce bias and trip up some ATS
  parsers, but it's not this tool's call to refuse.
- No color beyond a single dark neutral for headers/dividers, never in body text.
- Name and contact info live in the document body, never a header/footer margin — many ATS systems
  ignore those regions entirely.
- Standard web-safe fonts, standard sizing (name/headers/body distinctly sized), tight line and
  section spacing — all already baked into the templates; don't override them per-run.
- **Dates: one consistent format across the whole document.** Normalize the source CV's date
  ranges (e.g. "Jan 2022 – Present", "2022–Present") to a single style used everywhere; never leave
  a mix of formats. This is formatting, not tailoring — apply it regardless of the job.
- **Personal details**: never add age, date of birth, marital status, or nationality that isn't
  already in the source CV. If the source includes them, keep them only when they match the
  evident regional norm of the source (common on EU/Gulf-region CVs, not on US/UK ones) — when
  unsure, omit rather than add.

## Skills section
Skills is the fastest place a CV overflows — cut hard.
- **Hard cap: 20 items total**, grouped into up to 5 categories (or one flat list) — categories
  don't need to be even; fold near-empty ones into a related category. Tool/skill names only, no
  parenthetical scope explanations.
- Prioritize items that appear in the extracted JD-requirements list; drop items with no bearing on
  this posting first.

## Digital footprint
- Carry over LinkedIn/GitHub/portfolio links from the source CV into `links` — don't drop them for
  space before cutting anything else.
